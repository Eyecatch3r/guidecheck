# Web Surveys: A Practical Guide

*Synthetic demo guide for Guidecheck. This guide is not a real publication. It shows how a guide from 2014 looks after conversion to Markdown.*

## 1 Introduction

Web surveys are a fast and low-cost method to collect data from large samples. They also make complex questionnaires possible: filters, randomization, and multimedia content. This guide gives practical recommendations for researchers who plan a web survey in the social sciences.

## 2 Coverage and sampling

Internet access in the general population is still uneven. Older people and people with lower formal education are underrepresented among Internet users. A web survey alone is therefore not suitable for inference to the general population. Use a probability-based sample and offer an offline mode, or recruit offline and give equipment to households without Internet access.

Opt-in (nonprobability) online panels are useful for experiments and pretests. Do not use them to estimate population values.

## 3 Devices

Almost all respondents complete web surveys on a desktop or laptop computer. Completion on smartphones is rare, usually below 5 percent of cases. Researchers can therefore design the questionnaire for a large screen and ask smartphone users to switch to a computer.

Test the questionnaire in the two or three most common web browsers before fieldwork.

## 4 Questionnaire design

### 4.1 Question wording

Use short, simple questions. Avoid double-barrelled questions, double negatives, and technical terms. Give every point of a response scale a clear verbal label.

### 4.2 Layout

Grid (matrix) questions save space and reduce the time respondents need. Use grids for batteries of items that share the same response scale.

Sliders and visual analogue scales are a modern alternative to radio buttons and can increase respondent engagement.

Show a progress indicator on every page, so that respondents know how much of the questionnaire remains (Couper, 2008).

## 5 Response rates and reporting

Report the final outcome of all sampled cases. Calculate response rates according to the AAPOR Standard Definitions (7th edition, 2011).

## 6 Data quality

Include one or two instructed-response items (for example, "Please select 'strongly agree' for this item") to identify inattentive respondents. Respondents who fail these items can be excluded. These checks are sufficient to protect data quality in online panels.

## 7 Data protection

Obtain informed consent before the first question. Store personal data separately from survey answers. Follow the EU Data Protection Directive (95/46/EC) and the national data protection law.

## 8 Accessibility

Make the questionnaire usable with screen readers and with a keyboard only. Follow the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.0.
