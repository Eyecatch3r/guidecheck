## 3. Timeliness

The guide **requires substantive updating**. The coverage, sampling, and question wording advice is still current. The device, data quality, and data protection sections rest on facts that are no longer true. The interface shows the rating for each dimension A to H.

## 5. Revision level

**Substantive updating.** The structure of the guide still fits the topic. No section must be added or removed. Three sections need new arguments, not only new references.

## 6. Genuine advances versus citation accumulation

The device and data quality changes rest on convergent evidence: pooled data from 18 surveys, two experiments in probability panels, and two independent lines of work on fraudulent responses. The slider change rests on one experiment and is therefore rated relevant, not significant.

## 8. Editorial recommendation

**Verdict:** Substantive update.

**Essential:** c1 (devices), c6 (data quality), c7 (data protection), c9 (references).
**Important:** c2, c3, c5, c8.
**Optional:** c4.

**Editorial conclusion.** If this guide were prepared for publication today, an editor would first remove the device advice. It tells researchers to design for computers and to redirect smartphone users. Both recommendations now increase nonresponse risk for a large share of respondents, and the main reason for them, lower answer quality on smartphones, is not supported by experiments. Second, the editor would remove the statement that attention checks are sufficient. Evidence from opt-in samples and from a recent experiment with AI agents shows the opposite, and a methods guide that calls these checks sufficient gives false security. Third, the legal reference must change, because the cited directive has not applied since 2018. These three changes are about correctness, not style. The rest of the guide is in good condition: the sampling warnings, the question wording rules, and the consent advice remain well supported and must stay. The grid, slider, and accessibility updates make the guide more useful, but a reader who does not get them still receives sound advice.
